\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Digital screen recordings, graphical user interface (UI) screencasts, slide presentations, and stylized animations represent an increasingly dominant fraction of modern multimedia communication. However, contemporary video coding standards---such as H.264/AVC, H.265/HEVC, and AV1---are architected primarily for natural camera footage. They rely on block-based motion estimation coupled with two-dimensional spatial transform coding (such as the Discrete Cosine Transform) and chroma subsampling ($4:2:0$). When applied to synthetic screen content characterized by sharp, single-pixel text boundaries, abrupt color transitions, and prolonged static intervals punctuated by localized updates, standard hybrid codecs frequently introduce mosquito noise, edge blur, and high transform overhead.

This thesis introduces \textbf{TimeFlowCodec (TFC)}, a video compression paradigm based on \textit{per-pixel temporal function regression}. Rather than performing spatial block partitioning and two-dimensional frequency transforms per frame, TFC models the temporal luminance and chrominance trajectory $s_{x,y,c}(t)$ of each individual pixel (or micro-tile) over time $t \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}$ using continuous low-order parametric functions. We derive closed-form Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) solutions for \textsc{Constant} ($\hat{s}(t) = a$) and \textsc{Linear} ($\hat{s}(t) = a + bt$) temporal models that execute in $\mathcal{O}(T)$ streaming time. An adaptive decision engine selects between parametric models and a \textsc{Raw} or low-rank \textsc{Matrix} fallback mode based on a variance-normalized error ratio threshold $\tau$ and slope bound $\theta$.

We specify the .tfc container architecture, culminating in the v5 specification featuring bounded chunked streaming, 2-bit run-length encoded mode maps, uniform parameter quantization, exact rational frame rate timing, and multi-channel audio multiplexing. Furthermore, we develop a bounded-memory systems implementation with disk memory-mapping fallback, vectorized SIMD/array reconstruction, and an interactive desktop inspection suite. 

Experimental evaluations across four representative visual domains---Screen/UI captures, slide presentations, stylized animations, and natural camera video---demonstrate that TimeFlowCodec delivers superior preservation of high-frequency edges and text fidelity at high compression ratios on temporally coherent content, achieving 47.78~dB RGB PSNR and 0.993 SSIM on representative UI captures with instant decoding throughput exceeding 240 frames per second. We conclude with an information-theoretic analysis of the operating boundaries of temporal regression codecs and discuss extensions to orthogonal polynomial bases and GPU compute acceleration.
